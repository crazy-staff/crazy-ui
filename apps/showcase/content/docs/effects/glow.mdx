## Usage

Glow is a utility class from the theme, so there is nothing to import: add `glow` to any element.

```tsx
<div className="glow rounded-panel border border-border bg-surface">…</div>
```

It paints two radial gradients from the top (a stronger one at the left, a fainter one at the right) as
`background-image` only, so the element keeps its own background colour, border, radius and shadow. The
gradients fade out inside the element, so the glow is never cut off at an edge.

## Examples

### Page top

The light at the top of a page, as on this page. Put an absolutely positioned layer with `glow` at the top of
the page panel, behind the content (`-z-10` in an `isolate` parent), as in the preview at the top. In these
docs `<PageGlow />` does that for the whole page.

### Inside a frame

`glow` on a rounded frame fills it, for a call to action or an empty state. The frame's own background
shows under it.

<ComponentPreview name="glow/frame" />

### On a card

A card with `className="glow"` stands out from its neighbours, for the current release or a featured post.
Use it on one card in a group, not on all of them.

<ComponentPreview name="glow/card" />

### On a button

The `glow` prop of `Button` adds a soft accent shadow under a solid button. Keep it for the one main action
on a screen.

## Tokens

| Token | What it sets |
| --- | --- |
| `--cui-glow` | The stronger gradient, at the top left. |
| `--cui-glow-2` | The fainter gradient, at the top right. |
| `--cui-shadow-glow` | The shadow under a `glow` button (`shadow-glow`). |

Override them on `:root`, on `.dark` or on any element to change the colour or strength of the glow in that
subtree.

## Accessibility

Glow is decoration: it carries no meaning and needs no label. Text on a glowing background keeps the
contrast of the plain background because the gradients are light (20 % at the strongest point); check it
when you raise `--cui-glow`.
